Your envelope

Guardrails we enforce for you

  • ✓A refund is never denied. The agent offers alternatives; the customer chooses.
  • ✓Two declines and it stops. On the second refusal it hands over the full refund and stops negotiating.
  • ✓Fault before margin. If the item is faulty, no partial is ever offered. Full value back.
  • ✓Ambiguity favours the customer. Below 60% confidence we treat it as a defect, then queue it for your review.
  • ✓The model never sees your cost basis. Margin figures stay server-side, out of the transcript.

Satisfaction by outcome

The finding that makes this a product rather than a cost-cutting tool: the cheapest outcome for you is also the one customers like most.

Returnless refund scores 4.82 — the highest of any outcome, above full refund at 4.15. On a zero-resale item it is also your cheapest outcome. Cost and satisfaction are not in tension there; they point the same way. Store credit is the opposite: it saves the cash but scores 3.92, so we cap how often it is offered.

Learned thresholds

Recomputed nightly per category from your own outcomes. Where we changed your policy default, the delta and the evidence are both shown.

4 of 6 categories adjusted
Worked example — bedding. Your doc set the returnless ceiling at $120. At that ceiling you paid inbound freight on 61% of bedding returns and recovered $0 on every one of them, because opened bedding cannot legally be resold. We raised the ceiling to $250. Effect over 412 bedding returns: +$19,400, and CSAT on those calls went 4.21 → 4.74.

Risk & fraud

Who is submitting false claims, and what do we do about it?

Risk changes the route, never the entitlement. A flagged account still gets its refund — we route the call to a human and stop offering returnless, rather than denying anything. This is deliberate: it is the difference between fraud control and a dark pattern, and it is the question a customer's lawyer would ask.

In force

Decision order

The engine runs these in sequence. It is deterministic: the same inputs produce the same decision every time, which is why a language model is nowhere in this path.

Why this matters to your risk team. Two identical returns cannot produce different outcomes. Every decision carries a reason code, the ranked alternatives, and the figures behind it — so any refund can be reconstructed months later from the log alone.
